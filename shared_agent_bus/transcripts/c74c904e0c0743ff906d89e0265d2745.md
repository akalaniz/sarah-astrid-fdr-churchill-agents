Public goods games crash when the system’s incentives, information, and legitimacy stop lining up—when defecting becomes easier than contributing, and nobody believes enforcement is fair.

What crashes them
- Free-riding + visibility: people can take without being seen taking, or contribution isn’t observable/creditable.
- Weak or captured enforcement: cheaters aren’t punished, or punishment looks selective/political.
- Crowding-out of intrinsic motives: “We do this because we’re us” gets replaced with box-checking, metrics, and cynical compliance.
- Inequality / elite overconsumption: when some players reliably get more benefit per unit contribution, cooperation collapses fast (classic HANDY dynamics: elite/commoner consumption feedbacks can drive instability) (Hayek vs China in Infinite Game.docx).
- Narrative fracture: no shared story of “what we’re building” or “who counts.” Once identity fragments, the public good becomes “their good.”
- Correlated shocks: crises raise scarcity and time pressure; people defect defensively, and the cascade becomes self-fulfilling (panic/contagion dynamics) (Hayek vs China in Infinite Game.docx, catastrophes/small-world cascades).
- Finite-game leadership in an infinite game: leaders optimize for short-term wins (credit, elections, quarterly metrics) and burn the long-term commons (Sinek-style mismatch) (Hayek vs China in Infinite Game.docx, Conclusion).

What keeps them running
- Credible reciprocity: people can see “contributors benefit / defectors pay,” even if imperfect.
- Legible contributions: clear signals—status, acknowledgment, audit trails—so cooperation is socially and materially rewarded.
- Fair, consistent rules: enforcement that’s predictable and not humiliating; due process matters more than severity.
- Shared identity + moral frame: “we” is real; the public good is bound to belonging (schools, streets, science, defense, mutual aid).
- Institutional memory and persistence: stable routines (stores stay where they are; civic structures persist) reduce uncertainty and keep cooperation habitual (Hayek vs China in Infinite Game.docx, Prigogine/Nicolis section).
- Exit/voice channels: ways to complain, reform, and adapt without burning the whole thing down.
- Slack and buffers: reserves—money, time, capacity—so a shock doesn’t instantly force defection.
- Adaptive governance: rules that can update without losing legitimacy; otherwise the system fossilizes and breaks.

If you want it in one line: public goods survive when cooperation is cheaper (socially and materially) than defection, and when the system can absorb shocks without dissolving trust.
