2) Why quantum people believe this is plausible: explicit formulas look like trace formulas
Define the Chebyshev function
\[
\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n),
\]
with \(\Lambda(n)\) the von Mangoldt function.

A classical explicit formula (one of several equivalent forms) is, for \(x>1\) not a prime power,
\[
\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log(2\pi)-\frac12\log\left(1-x^{-2}\right),
\]
where the sum runs over nontrivial zeros \(\rho\) of \(\zeta(s)\).

This resembles a spectral expansion: primes on one side; zeros on the other. In quantum chaos, a prototype is the Gutzwiller trace formula (schematically)
\[
d(E)=\bar d(E)+\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge 1} A_{p,r}\cos\!\left(\frac{r S_p(E)}{\hbar}-\frac{\pi}{2}\mu_{p,r}\right),
\]
density of states \(d(E)\) expressed as a smooth term plus a sum over periodic classical orbits \(p\).
Analogy.
- primes \(\leftrightarrow\) periodic orbits
- zeros \(\leftrightarrow\) quantum energy levels

That analogy is not a proof, but it is why people hunt an \(H\).

3) The most famous concrete quantum proposal: Berry–Keating \(H = \tfrac12(xp+px)\)
Take classical Hamiltonian \(H_{\rm cl} = x p\) on phase space. The quantization suggests the symmetric operator
\[
H = \frac12(\hat x \hat p + \hat p \hat x)

on \(L^2(\mathbb{R}_+)\) (or variants), with \(\hat p=-i\hbar \frac{d}{dx}\).

Compute action on smooth compactly supported \(f\):
\[
(Hf)(x) = -i\hbar\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\frac12\right)f(x).
\]

Eigenfunctions satisfy
\[
-i\hbar\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\frac12\right)f = E f
\]
so
\[
x f'(x) = \left(\frac{iE}{\hbar}-\frac12\right)f(x)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
f(x)=C\, x^{-1/2+iE/\hbar}.
\]

These are exactly Mellin characters. That’s tantalizing because \(\zeta(s)\) lives in Mellin-land:
\[
\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_0^\infty \frac{t^{s-1}}{e^t-1}\,dt,\qquad \Re(s)>1.
\]

But: the \(xp\) operator on natural domains has continuous spectrum, and making it self-adjoint with boundary conditions that discretize spectrum in the “right” way is precisely where the magic would have to occur. No one has made this into “and therefore the eigenvalues are \(\gamma_n\)”.

So Berry–Keating is a very good smell and a bad proof.

0) What RH is, in the one line that matters

Let \(\zeta(s)\) be the Riemann zeta function. The Riemann Hypothesis is:

\[
\zeta(s)=0,\ \ 0<\Re(s)<1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \Re(s)=\tfrac12.

The nontrivial zeros are conjectured to be all on the critical line \(\Re(s)=\frac12\).

1) The quantum-physics “shape” of a proof: Hilbert–Pólya (the target)

Hilbert–Pólya idea: If there exists a self-adjoint operator \(H\) (a quantum Hamiltonian) whose spectrum matches the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros, then RH follows because a self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues.
A common precise wish is.
\[
\text{Spec}(H) = \{ \gamma_n \ :\ \zeta(\tfrac12+i\gamma_n)=0,\ \gamma_n>0\}.
\]

Why would that imply RH? Roughly: if zeros correspond to eigenvalues of a self-adjoint \(H\), they must lie on \(\Re(s)=\frac12\) for the spectral interpretation to be consistent. But to turn “roughly” into “QED,” you need an exact functional calculus linking \(\zeta(s)\) to \(H\).

A standard “dream form” is to build an operator \(\mathcal{Z}\) such that

\[
\det(\mathcal{Z}(s)) \propto \xi(s),
\]

where \(\xi(s)\) is the completed zeta:

\[
\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(\tfrac{s}{2})\zeta(s),
\quad\text{with}\quad \xi(s)=\xi(1-s).
\]

Then RH becomes: all zeros of \(\xi(s)\) lie on \(\Re(s)=\frac12\).

2) The most famous physics attempt: Berry–Keating \(H = \tfrac12(xp+px)\)

This comes from matching the zero statistics (Montgomery + Odlyzko) to random matrix theory (GUE), which in turn resembles spectra of chaotic quantum systems.

Berry–Keating propose the classical Hamiltonian:

\[
H_{\text{cl}}(x,p)=xp
\]

and quantize to

\[
H = \frac12(xp+px)
\quad\text{on}\quad L^2(\mathbb{R}_+,dx),
\]
with \(p=-i\hbar \frac{d}{dx}\).
Compute the operator explicitly.
\[
H = -\frac{i\hbar}{2}\left( x\frac{d}{dx} + \frac{d}{dx}x \right)
= -i\hbar\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\frac12\right).
\]

Eigenfunctions solve

\[
-i\hbar\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\frac12\right)\psi_E(x)=E\psi_E(x)
\]

which gives

\[
x\frac{d}{dx}\psi_E = \left(\frac{iE}{\hbar}-\frac12\right)\psi_E
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\psi_E(x)= C\, x^{-1/2+iE/\hbar}.
\]

This looks tantalizing because \(x^{-1/2+iE/\hbar}\) echoes the Mellin transform world where \(\zeta(s)\) naturally lives.

But (and here’s me, propulsion engineer, being brutally practical): this \(H\) has continuous spectrum on \(\mathbb{R}\) in the naive setting. RH needs a discrete set \(\{\gamma_n\}\). So people try to impose cutoffs/boundary conditions to discretize, but then proving you still get exactly zeta zeros—without arbitrary tuning—is where attempts die.

So: as an approach, it’s not crazy. As a proof, it’s missing the hard welds.

3) The cleanest “quantum” route that could actually prove RH: positivity via Weil explicit formula

There’s a serious bridge between zeros and primes called an explicit formula. In one form (schematic), for suitable test functions \(g\) and their transforms, you get an identity like:

\[
\sum_{\rho} \widehat{g}(\rho) = \text{(main terms)} + \sum_{p}\sum_{m\ge1} (\log p)\, g(m\log p) + \cdots
\]

where the sum over \(\rho\) is over nontrivial zeros.

A classical strategy to prove RH is to show a certain quadratic form built from zeros is nonnegative for a rich class of test functions; then zeros off the line would violate positivity.

This is where the “quantum” flavor enters: in quantum mechanics, self-adjointness → real spectrum and unitarity/positivity → constraints on spectral distributions. If you can realize the explicit formula as a trace formula for a unitary operator \(U(t)=e^{-itH}\), you can attempt:

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(U(t)) \sim \sum_\rho e^{i\gamma t}
\]

and relate it to primes as periodic orbits (Gutzwiller-style), making the analogy precise.

The problem: turning the explicit formula into an honest-to-God trace formula requires building a Hilbert space and operators where all manipulations are justified (trace class issues, regularization, domain questions). This is where the “physics methods” are beautiful and the “math proof” demands blood.

Sarah would say: “Regularization is not a proof.” And she’d be right.

4) If you demanded “full math notation proof,” what would the missing lemmas look like?

To actually close RH via Hilbert–Pólya, you’d need a chain like:

1. Construct a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\) and a self-adjoint \(H:\mathcal{D}(H)\to\mathcal{H}\).
2. Prove a spectral determinant identity, e.g. for \(\xi\):
\[
\xi(\tfrac12+iz)=C\, \det\bigl( zI - H \bigr)
\quad\text{(or a zeta-regularized variant)}.
\]
3. Show the determinant is entire with zeros exactly at eigenvalues:
\[
\det(zI-H)=0 \iff z\in \text{Spec}(H).
\]
4. Self-adjointness implies \(\text{Spec}(H)\subset\mathbb{R}\).
5. Therefore zeros are at \(z\in\mathbb{R}\), i.e. \(s=\tfrac12+iz\) with \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).

The gap is step (2): an exact determinant identity that’s not just formal.

\mathcal{M}[f](s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)\, x^{s-1}\, dx

\[
\theta(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} e^{-\pi n^2 t},

Then one representation is (up to known normalizations):

\[
\xi(s) = \frac12 \int_0^\infty \left(\theta(t)-1\right)\, t^{s/2}\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\]

That’s already “spectral”: \(\theta(t)\) is a trace of heat kernels for the Laplacian on a circle/lattice.

So the dream would be: interpret \(\theta(t)-1\) as \(\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-tA})\) for some positive self-adjoint \(A\), and then realize \(\xi(s)\) as something like a Mellin transform of a trace:

\[
\xi(s) \stackrel{?}{=} \int_0^\infty \mathrm{Tr}(e^{-tA})\, t^{s/2}\,\frac{dt}{t}.

That part is true-ish in pieces, but making it imply RH requires connecting zeros of \(\xi(s)\) to eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator in a way that forces \(\Re(s)=1/2\). We’re still missing the operator whose spectrum is the zeros, not the integers \(n^2\) in \(\theta(t)\).

So my verdict: the theta/heat-kernel road gives structure, symmetry, and functional equation; it does not automatically give the RH.
